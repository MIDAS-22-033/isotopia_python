Versions

  v2  reports DI mass instead of activity/yield; stable DIs supported
  v3  adds rarity weighting (--rarity, --rarity-basis)

1. RARITY DATA (for Brett / the database)

1.1 No region choice.
    The Rarity Index Structure diagram has a region step (China, Europe, U.S.),
    but the workbook holds one price per element, and the market behind that
    price varies by row (mostly U.S.; some European, Chinese, Canadian or global
    benchmark prices). Obtainability is U.S.-only. The script therefore has no
    --region option; non-U.S. prices are flagged instead.

1.2 Enriched and radioactive targets look too cheap.
    Only 9 isotope rows exist (deuterium, Tc-99m, U-233, U-235, Pu-242, Pu-244,
    Am-243, Cm-248, Cf-249) plus the "main isotope" rows of radioactive-only
    elements. Every other isotope uses its element's natural price. Example:
    Mo-100 is scored as natural molybdenum, though enriched Mo-100 costs far
    more; Mo-99 (itself radioactive) is also scored as natural Mo. Target rarity
    is understated, which favours enriched targets. Nearly every route currently
    carries an "element-level price used" flag for this reason.
    Fix: add isotope-specific prices for common enriched targets.

1.3 Obtainability exists only per element.
    So an isotope-level obtainability value is never available. This case is not
    flagged, since the flag would appear on every route and carry no information.

1.4 Technetium has no obtainability score.
    With --rarity-basis obtainability, Tc falls back to its price (flagged).

1.5 The workbook has formulas but no cached values.
    It was never recalculated and saved in Excel, so the script re-evaluates the
    formulas itself. Verified against a LibreOffice recalculation: all 102 price
    rows and 79 obtainability rows agree to ~1e-16. If the sheet's formulas or
    column headers change, RarityIndex must be updated to match.

1.6 The price index depends on the whole sheet.
    It is normalised between the cheapest and most expensive rows (isotope rows
    included), so adding or repricing any row shifts every index value.

1.7 New dependency: openpyxl.
    Needed only when --rarity is used; not one of the project's core packages
    (numpy, scipy). Alternative: export the two sheets to CSV.


2. RARITY WEIGHTING METHOD

2.1 Score = mass x (1 + dR)/2, with dR = R_DI - R_target in [-1, 1].
    Equal rarity (dR = 0) halves the score, so scores are not masses. Ranking is
    correct, but always read the unweighted mass column (m_EOC_g) for amounts.

2.2 Only the target (P1) and the DI are scored.
    Decay precursors and co-produced nuclides do not affect the weight.

2.3 Targets rarer than the DI are kept, not dropped.
    They are scored lower and flagged "target rarer than DI". The manual
    exclusion list (--exclude) still drops routes and works independently.


3. PHYSICS AND MODEL LIMITS (inherited from the ISOTOPIA port)
   Details: claude/equations_f90_findings.md

3.1 Beta-minus parents do not feed daughters during cooling (defect 4).
    End-of-cooling masses are underestimated for DIs fed by beta- decay, e.g.
    Tc-99m from Mo-99, and stable products made mostly through decay
    (Mo-100 -> Tc-100 -> Ru-100). Compare m_EOI_g where this matters.

3.2 Only one step of decay feeding; no branching ratios; parent feeding
    double-counted; no isomeric transition (defects 1-3).

3.3 Long-lived parents are numerically fragile (defect 6, the Ru-099 anomaly).

3.4 No isotopic purity for stable DIs.
    The reported mass is the DI alone. Other isotopes of the same element
    (including the target, if the same element) cannot be separated chemically.

3.5 Mass production rates use different units by beam type:
    g/(mA h) for charged particles, g/(g target h) for neutrons and photons.
    The script warns if --metric rate mixes them.


4. SEARCH SETUP

4.1 Target window A-5..A+5 is a chosen rule, not derived.
    Lighter targets that cannot reach the DI mass in one reaction
    (A_target + A_projectile < A_DI) are skipped with a warning.

4.2 The DI's own ground state is never used as a target
    (its inventory is unreacted target, not production). An isomeric DI from its
    own ground state (Tc-99 -> Tc-99m) is kept.

4.3 Low energies are mostly empty.
    The Bethe-Bloch stopping power is invalid below ~0.5 MeV (protons), and
    charged-particle cross sections start at 0.5 MeV, so the 1 keV lower bound
    yields no usable runs there.

4.4 Fixed target thickness and default density; Eback derived from them.
    No heat-load limit is applied.

4.5 Photons: only integer 1-200 MeV spectra. Neutrons: fixed reactor spectrum,
    one run per target, no energy scan.

4.6 Fixed irradiation time, cooling time and beam current (defaults 1 d, 1 d,
    1 mA); rankings can change with these.

4.7 Radiotoxicity and processing time are not part of the ranking yet.

6. STEP BEAM (--steps N), NEW IN v4

6.1 This is new physics, not part of ISOTOPIA.
    ISOTOPIA's equations assume one constant beam energy. The step beam uses a
    new solver: the matrix exponential per step (exact for piecewise-constant
    rates; the ORIGEN method, Croff ORNL-5621 1983; Moler & Van Loan, SIAM
    Review 45 (2003) 3) with full decay chains from files/decay (ENDF-6
    MF8/MT457). The constant-energy search (no --steps) is unchanged.

6.2 The two modes disagree for DIs fed by decay. TEAM DECISION NEEDED.
    Full chains include beta- feeding during cooling, which ISOTOPIA's model
    leaves out (defect 4). For Tc-99m from Mo-100 (protons, 0.05 cm, 1 d + 1 d):
      constant-energy search (ISOTOPIA model):  best ~20 MeV, 1.32e-6 g at EOC
      step-beam solver, 1 constant step:        best 42 MeV,  2.80e-6 g at EOC
    The step-beam numbers are the more physical ones, but this overlaps Brett
    and Abby's chain-physics work; the team should agree which model is the
    reference before results are quoted.

6.3 When steps help.
    For a DI made only directly from the target, the best schedule is a constant
    beam at the peak energy; the optimiser correctly returns that (+0.00% for
    most targets in the Tc-99m test). Steps pay off when a precursor and the DI
    peak at different energies: Mo-100 -> Tc-99m gained +7.2% with 42 MeV for
    16.9 h (Mo-99), then 22 MeV for 7.1 h (direct Tc-99m).

6.4 Step energies land on grid points.
    Rates are interpolated linearly between grid energies, so optima sit on grid
    points (42.001, 22.001 MeV at --de 2). Rerun with a finer --de around the
    optimum for sharper energies.

6.5 The optimiser is stochastic.
    differential_evolution is seeded (--seed, default 0) for reproducibility and
    starts from the best constant beam, so it never does worse than constant;
    a global optimum is not guaranteed for many steps. Rerun with other seeds
    to check.

6.6 What is held fixed.
    Total irradiation time, cooling time, beam current and target thickness are
    fixed; each step's Eback follows from the thickness. No cost or dead time for
    switching energy, no limit on the number of switches beyond N, no heat-load
    limit. Steps may repeat the same energy (the schedule then has fewer
    distinct steps).

6.7 Modelling approximations.
    - Products are not hit again by the beam (no double hitting).
    - Target burnup is spread uniformly over the whole target, though the
      beam only reaches part of it when it stops inside (burnup is tiny).
    - A radioactive target decays during irradiation here (ISOTOPIA ignores
      this), so --include-radioactive targets can differ from the constant
      search.
    - ISOTOPIA's isomer labels (g, m, n) are assumed to match the decay
      library's state numbers (LISO 0, 1, 2).

6.8 Decay data gaps.
    States without a decay record are treated as stable (chain truncated,
    warned). Fission and unmapped decay modes are losses (warned). A missing
    isomer record of a daughter falls back to its ground state.

6.9 Only charged particles (p, d, t, h, a).
    Photon spectra exist only at whole MeV, and the reactor spectrum has no beam
    energy, so g and n are skipped in step mode with a warning.

6.10 Rarity weighting doesn't change the schedule.
    The weight depends only on the target and DI, so it reorders targets but
    never changes a target's best steps.

6.11 Runtime.
    One ISOTOPIA run per grid energy per target, then the optimiser: about
    1.5 min for Tc-99m (11 proton targets, --de 2, 2 steps, 4 jobs).
